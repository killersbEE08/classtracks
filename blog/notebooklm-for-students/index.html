What is NotebookLM?

NotebookLM is a free AI-powered research and study tool from Google. Unlike a general chatbot that answers from everything it was trained on, NotebookLM works only from the sources you give it — your lecture notes, textbook chapters, slide decks, Google Docs, PDFs, a pasted website, even a YouTube lecture link or an audio file. You gather your material into a "notebook," and from that moment the AI becomes an expert in your course and nothing else.

That single design choice changes everything. Ask a question and NotebookLM answers in plain language, then shows a citation linking straight to the exact sentence in your source. It can also generate a summary, a structured study guide, an FAQ, a timeline, and an Audio Overview — a surprisingly natural podcast-style conversation about your notes that you can listen to while walking to class.

lightbulb

Think of NotebookLM as a research assistant who has read only your syllabus and refuses to bluff. It will not tell you about topics outside your sources — and that limitation is exactly why it is trustworthy.

Why NotebookLM beats a normal chatbot for revision

Chatbots like ChatGPT are brilliant generalists, but for studying your specific course they have a flaw: they answer from general knowledge and can invent facts that sound right. NotebookLM fixes this for revision in three ways:

  • It is grounded in your material. Answers come from your notes and readings, so they match what your professor actually taught — not a generic version from the internet.
  • Every answer is cited. One click jumps you to the exact passage, so you can verify instantly and stop trusting AI blindly. This is the habit that protects your grades.
  • It hallucinates far less. Because it is restricted to your sources, the confident-but-wrong answers that plague general chatbots are much rarer.

The trade-off is that NotebookLM will not help with anything outside what you upload. That is a feature, not a bug: for exam revision, you want the AI to stay inside the syllabus. For broad explanations or brainstorming, reach for a general chatbot instead — see our roundup of the best AI tools for students to pick the right one per task.

How to study with NotebookLM: 5 steps

Here is a repeatable workflow you can run for any subject:

  1. Create a notebook and add your sources. Start a new notebook and upload your material — lecture notes, textbook PDFs, slides, Google Docs, a YouTube lecture or an audio recording. Add everything for one topic or module so the AI has the full picture.
  2. Generate a summary and study guide. Use the built-in tools to create a summary, a study guide, an FAQ and a timeline. Reading these first gives you the shape of the topic before you go deep.
  3. Ask questions and check the citations. Ask in plain language — "explain the Krebs cycle from my notes" — then click the citations to jump to the exact passage and confirm the answer is right.
  4. Turn it into active recall. Ask NotebookLM to "write 15 exam-style questions from these sources, without the answers." Test yourself from memory first, then paste your answers back for feedback. This converts passive notes into active recall, the technique that actually builds memory.
  5. Generate an Audio Overview. Create a podcast-style discussion of your notes and listen while commuting, walking or doing chores — a painless way to add spaced repetition to dead time.

The best NotebookLM prompts for students

NotebookLM is only as good as what you ask it. These prompts get the most out of your sources:

GoalPrompt to try
Overview"Summarise these sources into the 10 most important points for an exam."
Self-testing"Write 15 exam-style questions from these sources, hardest first, without the answers."
Understanding"Explain [concept] from my notes in simple terms, then in exam-level detail."
Comparisons"Make a table comparing [X] and [Y] using only these sources."
Gap-finding"What key terms in these sources am I most likely to be tested on?"
Memory aids"Create flashcard-style Q&A pairs for every key definition."

Limits & using NotebookLM without cheating

NotebookLM's free tier is generous but not unlimited — there are caps on how many notebooks you can keep, how many sources each holds, and how many Audio Overviews you can make per day. A paid plan raises these, but for a single course the free tier is usually plenty. Two other things to remember: it only knows what you upload, so the quality of your revision depends on the quality of your notes; and while citations make it very reliable, it is not flawless — click the citation to confirm anything important.

On academic integrity, the line is the same as with any AI. Using NotebookLM to summarise your own notes, quiz yourself or find where a topic is covered is studying. Submitting its output as your own work, or using it in an assessment where AI is banned, is cheating. Attempt the thinking yourself first, use NotebookLM to check and reinforce, and always follow your institution's policy. Used this way, it is one of the safest and most powerful study tools available — and it is completely free to start.

Key takeaways

  • It is the best AI tool for studying your own course material — notes, PDFs, slides and lectures.

  • End prompts with "without the answers" so you test yourself before checking.
  • It is free to start; use it to study, not to submit, and verify important answers via the citations.

Is NotebookLM free for students?
Yes. NotebookLM has a free tier that is generous enough for everyday studying, with limits on the number of notebooks, sources per notebook and Audio Overviews per day. A paid plan raises those limits, but most students never need it for a single course.
How is NotebookLM different from ChatGPT?
The key difference is grounding. ChatGPT answers from its general training and can invent facts, while NotebookLM answers only from the sources you give it and cites the exact passage for every claim. That makes NotebookLM far more reliable for studying your own course material, and much less likely to hallucinate — but it will not answer questions your sources do not cover.
Does NotebookLM hallucinate or make things up?
Because NotebookLM restricts its answers to your uploaded sources and cites them, it hallucinates far less than a general chatbot. It is not perfect, so you should still click the citations to confirm an answer reflects the source — but grounding in your own material makes it one of the most trustworthy AI tools for revision.

It becomes cheating if you submit its output as your own work or use it in an assessment where AI is banned. Always follow your institution's academic integrity policy and do the thinking yourself.
