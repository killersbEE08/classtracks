How to Add Your College Timetable to Google Calendar

schedule 8 min read update Updated August 2026 verified By ClassTrack

Quick answer

To add your timetable to Google Calendar, there are three ways: (1) auto-sync from a timetable app like ClassTrack — the fastest and always up to date; (2) import an ICS/CSV file if your college or app exports one, adding every class in one go; or (3) add each class manually as a weekly recurring event with a location and a 10–15 minute reminder. Whichever you pick, your schedule then syncs to every device signed in to your Google account.

Why put your timetable in Google Calendar

A paper timetable or a screenshot buried in your camera roll can't remind you, can't follow you between devices, and can't warn you about a clash. Google Calendar can. Once your classes live there, you get:

  • Automatic reminders before every lecture, lab and seminar.
  • Cross-device sync — the same schedule on your phone, laptop, tablet and watch.
  • A clear week view that instantly shows free slots for study, work or society meetings.
  • Room and building details attached to each class so you never turn up to the wrong place.

Method 1 — Auto-sync from a timetable app (fastest)

If you'd rather not build events by hand, connect a timetable app to Google Calendar and let it do the work. With ClassTrack you enter your timetable once (or snap a photo of it and let AI read it), then turn on Google Calendar sync:

  1. Add your timetable in ClassTrack — type it in or import it from a photo.
  2. Open Settings → Google Calendar and tap Connect.
  3. Grant calendar access to your Google account when prompted.
  4. Done. Every class becomes a recurring event with reminders, and future edits sync automatically.

The big win: when a class time changes, you edit it once in the app and Google Calendar updates itself — no hunting through recurring events.

Method 2 — Add classes manually as recurring events

No export file and a short timetable? Adding weekly recurring events by hand takes about 10–15 minutes and works on both web and mobile.

  1. Open Google Calendar at calendar.google.com or in the app, and sign in.
  2. Create the first class. Click Create → Event, name it after the subject, and set the start and end time of the first session.
  3. Make it repeat weekly. Open the recurrence dropdown (it says Does not repeat) and choose Weekly on [day].
  4. Set an end date. Choose Custom → Ends on and pick the last day of term so classes stop during the break.
  5. Add the location. Put the room or building in the Location field.
  6. Add a reminder. Set a notification 10–15 minutes before.
  7. Repeat for every class and give each subject its own colour.
info

Have the same class twice a week (say Monday and Thursday)? Use Custom recurrence and tick both days so it's a single event series instead of two.

Method 3 — Import an ICS or CSV file

Many student portals and timetable apps can export your schedule as an .ics (iCalendar) or .csv file. That lets you load the whole term in one import:

  1. Export your timetable from your college portal or app as an .ics or .csv file.
  2. On desktop, open Google Calendar and go to Settings → Import & export.
  3. Select the file, choose which calendar to add it to, and click Import.
  4. Review the week view to confirm every class landed on the right day and time.
tips_and_updates

Import into a separate calendar (e.g. "Timetable") rather than your main one. If something looks wrong you can delete and re-import without touching your personal events.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • No end date on recurring events. Without one, classes keep appearing through holidays and next year. Always set Ends on.
  • Ignoring reading/exam week changes. Timetables shift mid-term — schedule a 2-minute check every few weeks, or use auto-sync so it's handled for you.
  • Everything one colour. Colour-coding by subject makes the week view readable at a glance.
  • Forgetting the room. Add the location so a tap gives you directions on your phone.
  • Editing one event instead of the series. When a class time changes permanently, choose This and following events, not just This event.

Key takeaways

  • Three ways in: auto-sync from an app, manual recurring events, or ICS/CSV import.
  • Auto-sync is fastest and stays up to date on its own.
  • Always add a term end date, a location and a reminder to each class.
  • Colour-code subjects and verify the week view before you rely on it.

Frequently asked questions

Can I import my whole timetable into Google Calendar at once?
Yes. Google Calendar imports ICS or CSV files, so if your college or a timetable app exports one you can add every class in a single import via Settings → Import & export. Otherwise, add each class as a weekly recurring event, or use an app like ClassTrack that syncs your timetable automatically.
How do I add a repeating class to Google Calendar?
Create the first session as an event, open the recurrence option and choose Weekly on the correct day. Set the recurrence to end on the last day of term so the class stops appearing during the break.
Will my timetable sync across my phone and laptop?
Yes. Google Calendar is cloud-based, so any class you add on one device appears on every device signed in to the same Google account — phone, laptop, tablet and smartwatch.
How do I get reminders before each class?
Add a notification to each event, usually 10–15 minutes before it starts. You can also set a default reminder for the whole calendar in Settings so every class inherits it automatically.
What is the fastest way to keep my timetable up to date?
Use a timetable app that connects to Google Calendar and syncs changes automatically. When a class time changes you edit it once in the app and the calendar updates itself, instead of editing recurring events by hand.
