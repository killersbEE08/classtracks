What “free” should actually mean

Plenty of “free” apps are really free trials, or free-but-useless until you pay. For a student on a budget, the bar should be: the core job works, forever, without a card. Bonus points if the free tier syncs across devices.

How to spot the catch

  • “Free trial” in the store listing. That's paid with a delay.
  • Core features locked. If the main job needs Pro, it isn't a free app.
  • Ads that block the workflow. A few ads are fine; interruptions during study aren't.
  • No free sync. If your data is stuck on one device unless you pay, factor that in.

Legitimate scholarship and discount tools are always free to use — if one asks for a fee to apply, walk away (more in finding scholarships & internships).

Are free student apps safe to use?
Reputable ones are. Check the developer, permissions and reviews, and prefer apps with a clear privacy policy. Avoid any app or site that charges a fee to apply for scholarships — that's a scam signal.
What's the difference between free and free-trial apps?
A free app lets you use its core forever without paying. A free-trial app is paid software with a temporary grace period, after which features lock or billing starts. Check the store listing for ‘free trial’ wording.
