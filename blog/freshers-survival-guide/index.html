Freshers Survival Guide

schedule 9 min read update Updated August 2026 verified By ClassTrack

Quick answer

Surviving first year comes down to a few things nobody tells you: protect your attendance from day one, keep every deadline in one place, budget from week one, say yes to societies early, and use the opportunities your student status unlocks. Get the systems right and the fun takes care of itself.

  2. Money without stress
  3. Making friends

Stay on top of academics from day one

  • Protect attendance early. The 75% rule sneaks up on first-years — build a buffer now. See how to improve attendance.
  • One place for every deadline. Assignments, labs and exams in a single app, not scattered across chats and sticky notes.
  • Learn to study, not just attend. Active recall and the Pomodoro Technique beat highlighting.
  • Know your GPA target. First-year marks often count — see what a good GPA is.

Money without the stress

  • Set a simple monthly budget and track spending against it — ClassTrack has this built in.
  • Claim student discounts on software, transport, food and tech; many aren't advertised.
  • Cook a few basics — the single biggest saving most students can make.

Making friends (the part that matters most)

  • Say yes early. The first few weeks are the easiest time to meet people — join societies and go to events.
  • Leave your door open (literally, in halls) and go to the group things even when you'd rather not.
  • Find your people, not everyone. A couple of good friends beats a huge contact list.

Use your student status

Being a student unlocks scholarships, internships, hackathons, free courses and discounts — most of which first-years never claim. A matched opportunities feed surfaces what you qualify for; learn the ropes in finding scholarships & internships.

Common freshers mistakes to avoid

  • Ignoring attendance until it's a problem.
  • Leaving assignments to the night before.
  • Blowing the budget in week one.
  • Skipping societies and regretting it later.
  • Not asking for help — tutors and wellbeing services exist for you.

Key takeaways

  • Protect your attendance and keep every deadline in one place from day one.
  • Budget from week one and claim the student discounts you're entitled to.
  • Say yes to societies early — the first weeks are the easiest time to make friends.
  • Use your student status for scholarships, internships and free courses.

Frequently asked questions

How do I survive my first year of college?
Get the systems right early: protect your attendance, keep every deadline in one place, budget from week one, join societies while it's easy to meet people, and use the scholarships and discounts your student status unlocks. Solid systems free you up to enjoy it.
What are common first-year mistakes?
Ignoring attendance until it's a problem, leaving assignments to the last night, overspending in the first weeks, skipping societies, and not asking tutors or wellbeing services for help. All are easy to avoid with a little structure.
How do I make friends as a fresher?
Say yes early — the first few weeks are the easiest time to meet people. Join societies, go to group events even when you'd rather not, and aim for a few genuine friendships rather than a huge contact list.
How do students save money in first year?
Set a simple monthly budget and track spending, cook a few basics instead of eating out, and claim student discounts on software, transport, food and tech — many of which aren't advertised to the general public.
What opportunities should first-years look for?
Scholarships, grants, internships, hackathons, free courses and student discounts — most of which first-years never claim. A matched opportunities feed shows what you qualify for so you don't miss deadlines.
