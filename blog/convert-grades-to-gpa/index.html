How to Convert Your Grades to a 4.0 GPA (Any Country)

schedule 9 min read update Checked 22 October 2026 verified By Prince Kumar

Quick answer

First, check whether you should convert at all — many universities want your grades on the original scale and convert them themselves, or ask for an official evaluation from a service like WES. If you need an estimate, don't just divide your mark by the maximum: in the UK a 70% is a First (about 3.7–4.0), not 2.8. For Germany, use the official modified Bavarian formula: German grade = 1 + 3 × (best − yours) ÷ (best − pass). The free converter below does both for 10 grading systems.

About 7.3 million students now study outside their home country, according to UNESCO — and millions more apply to exchange programmes, scholarships and graduate schools abroad. Almost all of them hit the same wall: the form asks for a "GPA on a 4.0 scale", and your transcript says 15.4/20, 1.7, 68% or 8.2 CGPA. This guide is for students everywhere — from Lagos to Lyon, Manila to Melbourne — and explains how conversion really works, so you report your grades accurately and confidently.

Should you convert your grades at all?

Often, no. Before you calculate anything, read the admissions or scholarship page carefully. You will usually see one of three instructions:

  1. "Report grades as they appear on your transcript." The most common rule for international applicants. Enter your original grade and scale (for example 15.2 / 20) and let the university convert it with its own tables.
  2. "Submit an official credential evaluation." Common for US graduate schools and some employers and licensing bodies. Services such as WES (World Education Services) or ECE convert course by course and send an official report — your own estimate doesn't count.
  3. "Enter your GPA on a 4.0 scale." Only here do you need to convert yourself — and you should still say it's an estimate and attach the original transcript.
fact_check

Why it matters: admissions officers see transcripts from dozens of countries every week. A self-converted GPA that looks inflated (a UK 2:2 reported as 3.8, say) damages your credibility far more than an honest original grade ever would.

Why simple division gives the wrong answer

The tempting shortcut is your mark ÷ maximum × 4. It works badly because every grading system has its own culture of marking:

  • United Kingdom: marks above 80% are rare in most subjects. A 70% is already a First Class — the top degree classification. Division would turn it into 2.8, which is wrong.
  • France: a common saying is that 20/20 is "for God" and 19 "for the professor". A 16/20 is excellent; division would make it 3.2.
  • Germany: the scale runs the other way — 1.0 is the best grade and 4.0 the lowest pass.
  • United States: grades are often higher on average, so 90%+ is a normal A range.

That's why credential evaluators use bands — "a UK 60–69 equals roughly a B+/A−" — instead of formulas. The pass mark matters too: in the UK it's usually 40%, in Spain 5/10, in the Netherlands 5.5/10, in Italy 18/30.

    Converting to the German scale (modified Bavarian formula)

    Applying to a university in Germany? Unlike the US, Germany has one official formula. It's set out by the KMK (the Standing Conference of education ministers) and used by universities and uni-assist:

    calculate

    German grade = 1 + 3 × (Nmax − Nd) ÷ (Nmax − Nmin)
    Nmax = best possible grade on your scale · Nmin = lowest passing grade · Nd = your grade

    Worked example: a UK student with 65% (best 100, pass 40): 1 + 3 × (100 − 65) ÷ (100 − 40) = 1 + 3 × 35 ÷ 60 = 2.75, which most universities cut to 2.7 ("befriedigend"). A US student with a 3.5 GPA (best 4.0, pass 1.0): 1 + 3 × 0.5 ÷ 3 = 1.5 ("sehr gut"). The converter above does this automatically. German universities usually keep only the first decimal, but some round instead, so check their admissions page.

    How to report your grades on an application

    1. Lead with the original. "First Class Honours (72%)", "15.4 / 20", "1.7 (German scale)". This is never wrong.
    2. Add the estimate only if asked, labelled clearly: "≈ 3.7 / 4.0 (self-estimated; official WES evaluation available on request)".
    3. Include context. Class rank, top-percentage statements or an ECTS distribution table help reviewers who don't know your system.
    4. Get an official evaluation when required — and start early, because evaluators need official transcripts sent directly from your university and can take weeks.
    5. Keep your own records accurate. Tracking every course grade as you go makes evaluations, scholarship forms and CVs much faster.

    Key takeaways

    • Check the application first — many universities want original grades or an official evaluation, not your own conversion.
    • Don't divide your mark by the maximum: strict systems like the UK and France would come out far too low.
    • Typical bands: UK First ≈ 3.7–4.0, 2:1 ≈ 3.3–3.7; French 14+/20 ≈ 3.7–4.0; Dutch 8+ ≈ 3.7–4.0.
    • For Germany, use the official Bavarian formula: 1 + 3 × (best − yours) ÷ (best − pass).
    • Always show your original grade alongside any estimate.

    Frequently asked questions

    How do I convert my grades to a 4.0 GPA?
    First check whether the university wants you to convert at all — many ask for original grades or an official evaluation such as WES. If you need an estimate, use your country's typical conversion band rather than simple division. For example, a UK First (70%+) is commonly treated as about 3.7–4.0.
    What is a UK 2:1 in GPA?
    A 2:1 (usually 60–69%) is commonly treated as about 3.3–3.7. A First (70%+) is about 3.7–4.0 and a 2:2 (50–59%) about 2.7–3.0. Universities use their own tables.
    What is the Bavarian formula?
    The official method German universities use to convert foreign grades: German grade = 1 + 3 × (Nmax − Nd) ÷ (Nmax − Nmin), where Nmax is the best grade on your scale, Nmin the lowest pass and Nd your grade. 1.0 is the best German grade and 4.0 the lowest pass.
    How do I convert a 10-point CGPA to a 4.0 GPA?
    A rough estimate is CGPA ÷ 10 × 4, so 8.0 ≈ 3.2. Evaluators such as WES convert course by course, so the official figure can differ.
    Should I convert my grades myself on an application?
    Only if the application asks. Otherwise report grades exactly as on your transcript, with the scale, and let the university or an evaluator convert them. An inflated self-conversion can hurt your credibility.
