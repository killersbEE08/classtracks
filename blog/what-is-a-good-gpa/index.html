What Is a Good GPA in College?

schedule 8 min read update Updated August 2026 verified By ClassTrack

Quick answer

On the standard 4.0 scale, a GPA of 3.5 or above is very good to excellent, 3.0–3.5 is good and solid, and 2.0 is usually the minimum to stay in good standing and graduate. The average college GPA in the US is roughly 3.1, so anything higher is above average. On a 10-point CGPA scale, 9.0+ is outstanding, 8.0–9.0 very good and 7.0–8.0 good. What actually counts as "good," though, depends on your goal — honors, scholarships, grad school or your first job each have a different bar.

What counts as a good GPA on the 4.0 scale

The most common answer students want is a single number, so here it is: on a 4.0 scale, a GPA above 3.5 is excellent, 3.0 to 3.5 is good, and 2.0 is the usual floor for staying enrolled and graduating. Because the average college GPA in the United States hovers around 3.1, crossing 3.5 puts you comfortably in the top tier of your class.

But "good" is not one fixed line — it is a range, and where you land carries real meaning. Here is how the numbers generally break down:

GPA (4.0 scale) Letter grade What it means
3.9 – 4.0A / A+Outstanding — top of the class, summa cum laude territory
3.7 – 3.9A−Excellent — competitive for grad school and honors
3.5 – 3.7B+ / A−Very good — dean's list and cum laude at many schools
3.0 – 3.5BGood and solid — meets most scholarship and employer minimums
2.0 – 3.0CAverage — passing, but below many program cut-offs
Below 2.0D / FAt risk — usually triggers academic probation
lightbulb

Thresholds like honors (cum laude ≈ 3.5, magna cum laude ≈ 3.7, summa cum laude ≈ 3.9) vary by institution. Always check your own school's published cut-offs rather than assuming — the numbers above are typical, not universal.

GPA scales explained: 4.0, 10-point and percentage

Not every country or university uses the same scale, which is why "what is a good GPA" has more than one right answer. The three you will meet most often are:

  • 4.0 scale (US and many others). Each letter grade maps to grade points: A = 4.0, B = 3.0, C = 2.0, D = 1.0, F = 0. Your GPA is the credit-weighted average of those points.
  • 10-point scale / CGPA (common in India and elsewhere). Grades map to a 10-point scale where 10 is the top. Here, 9.0+ is outstanding, 8.0–9.0 very good, 7.0–8.0 good and 6.0–7.0 average. A rough percentage conversion is CGPA × 9.5, but confirm your university's official formula.
  • Percentage scale. Some systems grade purely in percentages, where roughly 90%+ is outstanding, 75–90% is very good and 60–75% is good. First-class honours typically starts around 70%.

The key idea across all three: a "good" GPA is one that sits clearly above your system's average and clears the bar for whatever you are aiming at next. A 3.4 on a 4.0 scale, an 8.3 CGPA and a 79% are all describing roughly the same "solidly good" student.

A good GPA depends on your goal

Rather than chasing a perfect number, work backwards from what you actually need it for. The honest truth is that the "right" GPA is goal-dependent:

  • Staying in good standing: most schools require a cumulative GPA of at least 2.0. Fall below it and you risk academic probation.
  • Scholarships and financial aid: many merit awards require a 3.0–3.5 minimum, checked every semester or year.
  • Honor roll / dean's list: commonly 3.5+, though some schools set it at 3.7.
  • Graduate and professional school: most programs list a 3.0 minimum; competitive ones expect 3.5+. Law and medical school admissions skew even higher.
  • Your first job or internship: some large employers screen at 3.0, but this matters mostly for your first role — after that, experience wins.
insights

A rising GPA trend can matter as much as the final number. Admissions officers and recruiters notice a student who went from 2.8 in first year to 3.7 by graduation — it signals grit and growth, not just talent.

How to calculate your GPA

Your GPA is a credit-weighted average, not a plain average — courses worth more credits pull harder on your number. To calculate it:

  1. Convert each course grade to its grade points (A = 4.0, B = 3.0, and so on).
  2. Multiply each course's grade points by its credit hours to get quality points.
  3. Add up all the quality points, then divide by the total credit hours.

For example, an A (4.0) in a 3-credit course and a B (3.0) in a 4-credit course gives (4.0×3 + 3.0×4) ÷ 7 = 24 ÷ 7 = 3.43. How to improve your GPA

Whether you want to cross into honors or pull back from probation, the levers are the same. What separates students whose GPA climbs is not raw ability — it is where they put their effort:

  • Prioritise high-credit courses. Because credits are weighted, a strong grade in a 4-credit course moves your GPA more than the same grade in a 1-credit one. Protect the big ones first.
  • Study actively, not passively. Test yourself and space out revision instead of re-reading — see our guide on how to study smarter with AI.
  • Protect the easy marks. Attendance, participation and submitted assignments are often "free" points. Missing them quietly drags your GPA down — our attendance calculator helps you stay above the line.
  • Ask for help early. Office hours, tutoring and study groups fix small gaps before they become failed exams.
  • Use grade replacement. Many schools let you retake a low-grade course and replace the old mark. Check your policy — it can lift a cumulative GPA fast.
  • Track it every term. You cannot improve what you do not measure. Watching your GPA trend each semester tells you what is working.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good GPA in college?
On the standard 4.0 scale, a GPA of 3.5 or above is considered very good to excellent, 3.0 to 3.5 is good and solid, and 2.0 is usually the minimum needed to stay in good academic standing and graduate. The national average college GPA in the United States sits around 3.1, so anything above that is above average.
Is a 3.5 GPA good?
Yes. A 3.5 GPA on a 4.0 scale is well above the national average of roughly 3.1, typically places you on the honor roll or dean's list, and meets the entry requirement for most scholarships and graduate programs. It is often the threshold for cum laude honors.
What is a good GPA on a 10-point (CGPA) scale?
On a 10-point CGPA scale, a score of 9.0 and above is outstanding, 8.0 to 9.0 is very good, 7.0 to 8.0 is good, and 6.0 to 7.0 is average. Most institutions require a minimum CGPA of around 5.0 to 6.0 to pass. A quick rough conversion to a percentage is CGPA multiplied by 9.5, though the exact formula varies by university.
What GPA do you need for grad school?
Most graduate programs set a minimum GPA of 3.0 on a 4.0 scale, while competitive programs typically expect 3.5 or higher. Strong recommendations, research or work experience and test scores can offset a slightly lower GPA at many schools.
Does GPA matter for jobs?
GPA matters most for your first job or internship, where some employers screen for a minimum of 3.0. After you have a year or two of work experience, most employers care far more about your skills, portfolio and results than your GPA.
How can I improve my GPA?
Focus your effort on high-credit courses, use active recall and spaced repetition instead of re-reading, attend classes to protect participation marks, ask for help early, and retake low-grade courses where your institution allows grade replacement. Tracking your GPA each semester helps you see what needs to change.
