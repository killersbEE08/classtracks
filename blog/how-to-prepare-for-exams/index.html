How to Prepare for Exams in 7 Days

schedule 8 min read update Updated August 2026 verified By ClassTrack

Quick answer

To prepare for exams in 7 days: audit and prioritise topics (red/amber/green by confidence and weight), front-load the hard ones, study with active recall and practice questions (not re-reading), then run timed past papers in the last two days and sleep the night before. First: audit and prioritise

Before studying anything, spend 30 minutes listing every topic and rating each red (shaky), amber (okay) or green (solid). Weight by how much each topic is worth in the exam. You'll study the reds and heavy-weight topics first — not whatever feels comfortable.

The 7-day plan

DaysFocusMethod
1–2Hardest / highest-weight topicsLearn, then self-test
3–4Remaining reds and ambersActive recall + practice questions
5Weak spots surfaced so farTargeted review
6Full past paperTimed, exam conditions
7Light review + restSkim summaries, sleep early

Study in Pomodoro blocks and split your hours across subjects with the study planner.

Techniques that work under pressure

  • Active recall: close the book and write what you remember; check; repeat. Far better than re-reading.
  • Past papers: the single highest-yield activity — they train exam technique and reveal gaps.
  • Spaced repetition: revisit a topic the next day and again later in the week.
  • Teach it: explaining a topic out loud exposes what you don't actually know.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Re-reading notes for comfort — it feels productive but barely helps recall.
  • Starting with easy topics — spend your best energy on the hardest first.
  • All-nighters — lost sleep costs more marks than the extra hours gain.
  • No past papers — knowing content isn't the same as answering exam questions.

Key takeaways

  • Prioritise by confidence and exam weight before you study anything.
  • Front-load the hardest, highest-weight topics in the first days.
  • Active recall and timed past papers beat re-reading every time.
  • Keep the last day light and sleep — all-nighters lose more than they gain.

Frequently asked questions

Can I prepare for an exam in 7 days?
Yes, if you prioritise ruthlessly. Rank topics by confidence and exam weight, front-load the hardest ones, use active recall and practice questions rather than re-reading, and do timed past papers in the final two days.
What is the most effective way to revise?
Active recall combined with spaced repetition and past papers. Testing yourself and answering real exam questions strengthens memory and technique far more than passively re-reading notes.
Should I pull an all-nighter before an exam?
No. Losing sleep hurts memory, focus and problem-solving, usually costing more marks than the extra study time earns. Keep the last day light and sleep well.
How many hours a day should I study in exam week?
Most students sustain 4–6 focused hours a day in Pomodoro blocks. Beyond that, returns drop sharply. Protect breaks and sleep so the focused hours stay high-quality.
How do I stop panicking about an exam?
Turn the vague worry into a concrete plan: know your days left with a countdown, list and rank topics, and book fixed study blocks. Visible progress against a plan is the fastest way to reduce panic.
