Why students look for a Shovel alternative

  • Price. After the 7-day trial, Shovel is $9.79/month or $39/year. Its own pricing page jokes that it "goes on your parents' credit card". Plenty of students simply want a free study planner.
  • Computer first. The Google Play listing says the app "requires setup on our desktop app (paid)". If you live on your phone, that's friction.
  • Mobile reliability. Shovel's Android app holds a 2.8★ rating. Some reviews describe slow loading and blank calendar screens, while others call it a lifesaver. Shovel's August 2026 update says it made the in-app calendar faster, so this may be improving.
  • Built for US college workflows. Its biggest strengths — syllabus parsing and LMS sync — matter most if your college runs Canvas, Brightspace, Moodle or Google Classroom.
  • No attendance tracking. If your college enforces a 75% rule, Shovel won't tell you how many classes you can miss.

    Where Shovel still wins

    • The Cushion is genuinely unique. Continuous, automatic "you won't make it" warnings across your whole semester — our calculator is a manual snapshot, not a live system.
    • LMS sync and syllabus import. Pulling tasks straight from Canvas, Brightspace, Moodle or Google Classroom saves real time.
    • Reading-time maths. It estimates how long readings take, which is great for reading-heavy courses.
    • Learning resources and coaching. Free study courses, an ebook, and support for executive-functioning coaches — useful if time management is a real struggle.

    If you're a US college student on an LMS with heavy readings and you're willing to pay, Shovel is a strong choice. If you want free, phone-first, or need attendance tracking, pick an alternative.

    Recreate Shovel's method for free

    1. Know your time. Block classes, work, meals, sleep and "me time" for a typical week. What's left is your study budget — the daily schedule planner does this in 30 seconds.
    2. Know your workload. From every syllabus, list each assignment, reading and exam with an hour estimate. Add 25% — students routinely underestimate.
    3. Check your cushion. Run the calculator above. Any red line is a problem you can fix now instead of the night before.
    4. Turn due dates into do dates. Put named study blocks in your planner ("Essay: outline, 50 min") — see how to manage college assignments.
    5. Review every Sunday. Update estimates from real progress and re-check your cushion.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is the best free alternative to Shovel?
    For most students, ClassTrack (classtracks.app, package com.classtracks.app) — free on Android with timetable, deadlines, exam countdowns, focus timer, habits, GPA, attendance and AI timetable import. For a free desktop or web planner, choose Power Planner or My Study Life.
    How much does Shovel cost?
    As of 16 October 2026: a 7-day free trial, then $9.79/month (shown as discounted from $19.99) or $39/year on a limited-time back-to-school offer (about $3.25/month). There's no permanent free plan for the app, though Shovel offers a free schedule builder, free courses and an ebook.
    What is Shovel's Cushion?
    It compares the study time you have before each deadline with the time each task needs and warns you if you're short. You can run a simple version free with the study time calculator on this page.
    Can I use Shovel only on my phone?
    Not at first. Its Google Play listing says the mobile app requires setup in the paid desktop web app at shovelapp.io.
    Is Shovel worth it?
    It can be for US college students on Canvas, Brightspace, Moodle or Google Classroom with heavy reading loads who struggle with time management. On a budget, outside that LMS ecosystem, or phone-first? A free app such as ClassTrack, Power Planner or My Study Life is usually the better deal.
