How to use the time you have

The number the tool gives you should change how you study today:

  • 3+ weeks: plenty of time to learn properly. Spread topics out and use spaced repetition.
  • 1–2 weeks: prioritise high-weight topics and start active recall (testing yourself), not re-reading.
  • Under a week: focus on past papers, practice questions and your weakest areas. See how to prepare for exams in 7 days.
  • The day before: light review, organise materials, sleep. Cramming overnight hurts more than it helps.

A simple countdown study plan

Once you know your days left, split the workload so you are not relying on a last-minute sprint:

  1. List every topic and rate each one red, amber or green by confidence.
  2. Assign reds and ambers first across your available days.
  3. Book fixed study blocks in your calendar and study in 25-minute focused sessions.
  4. Leave the last two days for full past-paper runs under timed conditions.

Want the whole term mapped out, not just one exam? Use the semester planner and the study planner.

Key takeaways

  • Knowing the exact days left turns “soon” into an actionable plan.
  • Start focused revision 2–3 weeks out; ramp up in the final week.
  • Use active recall and past papers as the exam nears, not passive re-reading.
  • Keep the day before light and prioritise sleep.

Frequently asked questions

How many days until my exam?
Enter your exam date in the countdown tool above and it shows the exact number of days and weeks remaining, updating live. It also tells you roughly how many weeks that works out to so you can plan your revision.
How far in advance should I start studying for an exam?
For most exams, begin focused revision about two to three weeks ahead. That gives you time to use spaced repetition and active recall rather than cramming. For heavier subjects, start earlier.
Is cramming the night before effective?
Rarely. Last-minute cramming can help you memorise a few facts but hurts recall and problem-solving because it costs you sleep. A light review plus a good night's sleep usually beats an all-nighter.
How do I plan study time around a countdown?
List your topics, rank them by confidence, assign the weakest ones to your earliest free days, and book fixed 25-minute study blocks. Reserve the final couple of days for timed past papers.
